# Competitive research: incremental and mobile idle, 2026

Purpose: place HERO//HQ against the games players actually compare it to, and
decide what to build next. Researched 2026-11-03 from the sources listed at the
bottom. Rankings are editorial or store-signal composites, not a single
authoritative chart — where two lists disagree, both are shown.

## Method and limits

- Two ranked lists were read in full: *Idle Distiller*'s 13 best incremental
  games and 13 best mobile idle games (Oct 2026), and *HeyCash*'s 10 best mobile
  idle games (Sep 2026, store ratings and monetisation taken from Google Play).
- Steam and mobile market context comes from AppMagic's 2026 idle research
  (revenue, copies, retention) and *Top Incremental Games*' mobile guide.
- Retention and monetisation figures come from Game-Ace's 2026 production guide
  and Segwise's 2026 benchmark table. They disagree on absolute levels; both are
  quoted because the gap itself is informative.
- Nothing here is proprietary data. No competitor was played for this note, so
  claims about feel are the sources' claims, not first-hand ones.

ones with a strong theme plus a collection hook.

Two facts stand out. **Eight of ten run ads**, and the two that do not (AFK
Arena, Melvor) have the largest and smallest rating samples respectively — so
"no ads" alone does not predict rating. Second, the list is dominated by
*collection and fantasy*, not by incremental purity: six of ten are RPG, tycoon
or collection games wearing an idle loop.

A second editorial list (*Top Incremental Games*, Sep 2026) ranks very
differently — **The Tower: Idle Tower Defense** first, then AdVenture
Capitalist, Leaf Blower Revolution, Idle Obelisk Miner — favouring small,
ad-light, single-developer games. The divergence is the finding: there are two
mobile markets, the ad-funded corporate idle and the ad-light systems idle, and
they barely overlap.

## Market context

- Mobile idle revenue peaked in 2022 and cooled after mid-2023 (AppMagic). Gold
  and Goblins alone passed 69M downloads and $163M in IAP; Idle Lumber Empire
  passed $113M. The hits are large but the format is crowded.
- Steam idle is the growth story: IdleOn made over $22M in 2025, Spirit City and
  Cast n Chill each passed $5.3M, The Farmer Was Replaced passed $4.5M with
  **D30 retention above 10%**, unusual for the genre. Most Steam idles are priced
  $3–15.
- Retention benchmarks disagree by source: Game-Ace quotes healthy soft-launch
  idle at **35–45% D1, 15–20% D7, 5–10% D30**; Segwise's 2026 cross-genre medians
  are **22% D1, 4% D7, under 1% D30**, with RPG at 30.5/9.9/3.5 and simulation at
  30.1/8.7/3.0. Plan against the higher idle-specific band, measure against the
  median.
- Curves: Game-Ace reports cost growth of **1.07–1.15 per upgrade level** for
  mobile idle, with prestige giving a **2–4× speedup on the second run**, offline
  caps commonly **2–24 hours**, and rewarded video (15–30 s) used for a **2×
  offline multiplier**. HERO//HQ currently runs 1.4 cost growth and a 4.6×-style
  first-chip jump — steeper than the mobile norm, closer to the browser
  incrementals.
- ARPDAU for idle titles sits around **$0.05–0.25**, upper band driven by LiveOps
  rather than installs.

## What HERO//HQ already does better

1. **No ads at all.** Eight of ten mobile leaders run ads; the genre norm is
   rewarded video for offline doubling. Our rule forbids even that, which is a
   defensible premium position and a marketing line, not just a constraint.
2. **One deterministic settlement path.** Offline and online completions share a
   single call, capped at 8 hours (market: 2–24 h). Competitors routinely have
   divergent offline formulas.
3. **A measured pacing gate.** `long_run_report.gd` fails the build when any wait
   exceeds 30 minutes. Almost no competitor ships a boredom budget as a test.
4. **No energy timers, no FOMO, no pay-to-win.** The sources' main complaints
   about mobile idle are exactly these.
5. **Pack-driven content.** Curves, tasks and prestige are data, so a retune does
   not require a client build — the thing Game-Ace calls remote config, we have
   by construction.

## What to do better, in order

### P0 — close the structural gaps

1. **A second meta layer, with Intel as its currency.** Every top-tier
   incremental has at least two prestige layers (Antimatter Dimensions, NGU,
   Trimps, Realm Grinder); we have one, and Intel currently has no sink. Give
   Intel a permanent "Research" tree: ranks that raise task payouts, shorten
   durations, or unlock automation, bought with lifetime Intel. This adds depth
   without new art and gives the 8 tasks a second axis.
2. **Automation as a milestone reward.** The highest-retention premium idles
   (The Farmer Was Replaced, Melvor, NGU) make automation the reward. Add
   "repeat last operation" per hero at a level gate, then auto-train. It removes
   the tap chore that makes short sessions feel like work.
3. **Roster depth and collection.** Six of ten mobile leaders are collection
   games. One hero is the single biggest content gap: 3–5 starters with distinct
   roles, recruitment, and squad missions. This is also what makes the reboot
   loop (prestige) feel different each run.
4. **A real welcome-back summary.** Tideward's model — itemised offline report,
   transparent math, no ad-to-double — matches our rules and is better than our
   silent settlement. We already produce the events; show them on return with a
   claim button.
5. **Engineer the first session's three "aha" moments.** Game-Ace's target: first
   automation, first big multiplier, first offline check-in inside session one.
   Add a tooling assertion that all three land before 15 minutes on a fresh save.

### P1 — differentiation and polish

6. **Rotating city events without FOMO.** Weekly modifiers (Intel surge, faster
   responses, a rare task) that *add*, never expire progress or punish absence.
   This is LiveOps-lite delivered through the pack, no server needed.
7. **Legibility pass.** Compact number notation, "per minute" on every payout,
   and an explicit "next unlock" line. Mobile leaders push numbers to the front;
   our screens are readable but not yet scannable.
8. **Prestige ETA.** We show the next-chip bar; add the modelled time to the next
   chip from the same formula the tooling uses, so the reset decision is informed.
9. **One-handed, accessible play.** 48 px targets are in place; add a
   colour-blind-safe check on the palette, reduced-motion, and a text-scale
   option. Reviews of ad-heavy competitors consistently punish clutter.
10. **Lean into the theme.** Antimatter Dimensions' stated weak spot is "no theme
    to hold onto". HERO//HQ has a superhero agency: names, banter, a city that
    reacts. Cheap narrative beats are a real advantage over pure-number rivals.

### P2 — later, and only if the above lands

11. **Optional premium unlock** (cosmetic or "supporter") as the monetisation
    path that respects the no-ads rule; revisit at M6 with real device data.
12. **Optional account + server timestamp** for cross-device saves. Note this
    conflicts with offline-first; treat as opt-in, never a gate.
13. **Seasonal cosmetic layer** keyed to `content_version`, no timed exclusives.

## Anti-patterns to keep refusing

- Energy systems and session gates (the top complaint in mobile idle reviews).
- Ads that double offline earnings; we forbid it, and the sources describe it as
  the norm precisely because it monetises impatience.
- FOMO events that delete progress or punish a week away.
- Late-game spreadsheet sprawl (Egg, Inc.'s cited weak spot: artifact
  combinations) and tap-fests (its drone chasing). Depth should stay legible.
- Content that only adds bigger numbers: the genre's "creativity problem" is
  node-buster clones, and our pack framework is the antidote if we use it.

## Open questions

- Which mobile market are we actually entering: the ad-funded corporate idle
  (AFK Arena, Idle Miner) or the ad-light systems idle (The Tower, Melvor)? The
  no-ads rule points at the second, which is smaller but fits our loop.
- Is a second meta layer enough, or does the next milestone need squads to make
  runs feel different? Collection depth is the stronger signal from the mobile
  top ten.
- What is our D1 target? The idle-specific band (35–45%) is aspirational; the
  cross-genre median (22%) is the floor.
